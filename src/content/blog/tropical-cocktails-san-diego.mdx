San Diego's coastal setting makes tropical cocktails an obvious fit, but a good tropical drink is more than fruit juice and a decorative garnish. Balance still matters: spirit, citrus, sweetness, dilution and texture have to work together just as they would in any other cocktail style.

In Mission Beach, [Miss B's Coconut Club](/menu) builds its bar program around tropical flavors in a setting designed to match them: an open-air bar, outdoor patio and Mission Blvd location steps from the Pacific.

## What Makes a Tropical Cocktail Work?

### A clear base spirit

Rum is common in tropical drinks, but it should still taste like a cocktail rather than disappear behind sweet ingredients.

### Fresh acidity

Citrus is usually what keeps tropical flavors from becoming heavy. Lime, lemon and other acidic components create structure.

### Fruit used with restraint

Guava, mango, coconut and pineapple can add flavor without turning a drink into a dessert when the proportions are balanced.

### A drink that fits the setting

A Mission Beach cocktail can be refreshing and relaxed without sacrificing technique. The point is to build something people want to drink outside on a warm afternoon, not simply something that photographs well.

## Tropical vs. Tiki: Not the Same Thing

The terms are often used interchangeably, but they are not identical.

**Tiki** usually refers to a specific American bar and cocktail tradition with its own history, visual language and classic drink canon.

**Tropical cocktails** is a broader description that can include rum-forward, fruit-driven and coastal drinks without adopting a full tiki-bar identity.

Miss B's is better described as a **tropical, Caribbean-inspired Mission Beach bar** than as a traditional tiki bar.
