A perfect beach day isn't just about sand and waves—it's about the full rhythm of the day, from sunrise swims to sunset cocktails. Mission Beach offers that complete experience, and Miss B's Coconut Club sits right in the middle of it all at 3704 Mission Blvd, making it the natural anchor point for a day that flows from boardwalk to bar to fire pit.

This guide walks you through the exact steps to plan a beach day that combines unstructured ocean time with strategic food and drink stops, all without feeling rushed or overcomplicated.

## Step 1: Arrive Early and Secure Parking

Mission Beach parking is competitive, especially on weekends. Arrive before 10 a.m. on weekdays or by 8 a.m. on Saturday or Sunday if you want street parking within a five-minute walk of the water or Miss B's. The lots near the lifeguard towers fill by mid-morning, and you'll spend 20 minutes circling if you show up after noon.

Park near Mission Boulevard itself rather than trying to squeeze into the beach lots. You'll actually walk less, and you'll be positioned perfectly to hit Miss B's later without moving your car. The restaurant sits directly on Mission Blvd with easy street access, so you won't need to backtrack.

## Step 2: Hit the Beach Early (8 a.m. to 11 a.m.)

Spend your first two to three hours in the water. The waves are typically smaller in the morning, the sun isn't intense yet, and you'll have room to move. Bring a reusable water bottle and sunscreen—you'll need both.

Mission Beach has a long, open coastline with consistent sandbars. The north end near the Belmont Park area tends to be slightly less crowded than the south end early in the day. If you're not a strong swimmer, stay in the designated beach areas and pay attention to lifeguard flags.

## Step 3: Dry Off and Grab a Light Snack (11 a.m. to 12:30 p.m.)

After your ocean session, you're hungry but not ready for a full meal. This is when Miss B's walk-up counter becomes your best friend. Order a Rum Runner or a Pineapple Punch—both are refreshing, not too heavy, and pair perfectly with the salt still on your skin. Grab some conch fritters or crispy fish tacos from the counter menu.

The walk-up system means no host stand, no table wait, no commitment. You order at the bar, take your plate, and either eat at a high-top near the counter or head back to the beach or boardwalk. Total time: 15 to 20 minutes.

## Step 4: Explore Mission Beach Beyond the Sand (12:30 p.m. to 3 p.m.)

With food in your stomach and a cocktail buzz going, spend the next few hours exploring the neighborhood. Walk the Belmont Park boardwalk, ride the Giant Dipper roller coaster if the mood strikes, browse the vintage shops and local boutiques along Mission Boulevard, or rent a bike and cruise the bay path.

This window gives you a break from the sun without leaving the beach vibe. You're still in the Mission Beach ecosystem, just experiencing it differently. By 3 p.m., you're naturally drawn back toward Miss B's as happy hour begins.

## Step 5: Settle Into Happy Hour (3 p.m. to 6 p.m.)

This is the sweet spot. Happy hour at Miss B's runs from 3 to 6 p.m. daily, with $2 to $4 off cocktails and select appetizers. The crowd is thinner than dinner service, the fire pit patio is open, and the light is still good.

Claim a spot at the bar or grab a table on the patio if one's available. Order a second round—try the Coconut Club Punch or a classic Mojito. Share a plate of jerk chicken wings or nachos with friends. The energy is relaxed but social; you're not fighting for attention, and the staff has time to chat.

If you're planning a group outing, this is also the ideal time to check if the fire pit patio has availability for later. While it's first-come, first-served, arriving early during happy hour gives you the best chance of securing a spot for sunset.

## Step 6: Transition to Dinner or Extended Happy Hour (5:30 p.m. to 8 p.m.)

As happy hour winds down around 5:30 p.m., decide whether you want to stay for dinner or move on. If you're staying, the dinner menu opens up—larger plates, more substantial dishes, full pricing. The fire pit patio becomes the premium spot as the sun moves lower, and the tiki-club energy picks up.

If you're heading out, this is a natural exit point. You've had a full day: ocean time, exploration, happy hour, and a buzz. You can leave feeling satisfied without overextending.

If you're staying for dinner, claim that fire pit patio table now. The flames come up as dusk hits, the temperature drops, and the whole vibe shifts toward evening. This is when Mission Beach transitions from beach day to night out, and Miss B's is the perfect place to make that transition.

## Step 7: Plan Around the Tides and Weather

Check the tide report before you go. Low tide means more sand to walk on and easier paddling if you're surfing. High tide can make the beach narrower and the water rougher. The National Weather Service San Diego page updates hourly.

Also check the swell forecast. Mission Beach doesn't get huge waves, but even small swell changes the water conditions. A 2-foot swell feels completely different than a 4-foot swell, especially for beginners.

Bring a light layer or beach cover-up. Even though it's warm during the day, the ocean breeze picks up in the late afternoon, and you'll want something to throw on while you're sitting at Miss B's happy hour.

You can hit a fish taco stand for lunch, grab ice cream at Baked Bear, or sit down for dinner at one of the seafood restaurants along the boulevard.

## Step 9: Consider a Group or Special Occasion

If you're planning a beach day for a birthday, bachelor party, or friend reunion, timing matters. Weekday afternoons are quieter and easier to navigate. Weekends are louder and more crowded but have more energy. If you want guaranteed seating or a reserved area, check the [private events mission beach](/blog/private-events-mission-beach/) guide or contact Miss B's directly about group options.

The fire pit patio works well for small groups (4 to 8 people) on a first-come basis. For larger parties, planning ahead ensures you get the experience you want.

## Step 10: End Your Day Right

By 8 p.m., you've been out for 10+ hours. You're sun-tired, fed, and buzzed. This is the natural endpoint for most beach days. If you're staying longer, you're shifting into full night mode—which is fine, but it's a different activity.

Most people heading home at this point will grab one more drink at Miss B's, watch the last light fade over the water, and head out satisfied. You've hit every phase: ocean, exploration, happy hour, and fire pit dinner. You've used the walk-up counter for quick refueling and the patio for extended relaxation. You've experienced Mission Beach as a full day, not just a beach visit.

If you're planning a beach day at Mission Beach and want to build it around Miss B's as your food and drink hub, this framework gives you the structure and flexibility to make it work. The walk-up counter keeps you moving when you want speed. The happy hour pricing rewards afternoon timing. The fire pit patio anchors the evening.
