Happy hour at the beach should feel intentional, not rushed. Miss B's Coconut Club—sitting right on 3704 Mission Blvd with ocean views and a roaring fire pit—makes it easy to slip into island time the moment you arrive. But showing up without a plan means you might miss the best drink specials, fight for seating, or overlook the shareable plates that pair perfectly with a mai tai. This guide walks you through exactly how to time your visit, what to order, and how to claim your spot before the crowd peaks.

## Step 1: Check the Current Happy Hour Schedule

Before you head down to Mission Beach, confirm Miss B's happy hour timing. Seasonal changes and special events can shift start and end times, so a quick call or Instagram check saves you from arriving when specials have ended.

Most beach bars cluster happy hour into the 4–6 p.m. window on weekdays, but Miss B's may extend into evening hours depending on the season. Summer Fridays often draw crowds earlier, so weekend timing differs from Tuesday afternoons. The bar staff can tell you exactly when top-shelf pours move to happy hour pricing and when food specials kick in.

## Step 2: Plan Your Arrival Time Around Crowd Flow

Timing is everything on Mission Beach. Arrive too early and you'll have your pick of the fire pit patio—but the bartender might still be setting up. Arrive during peak hours (5–7 p.m. on weekdays, 4–6 p.m. on weekends) and you'll compete for seating with office workers and tourists.

The sweet spot is 15–20 minutes after happy hour officially starts. The bar is fully staffed, the special pricing is live, and the patio still has open tables. If you're coming on a Friday or Saturday, aim for 4:15 p.m. to beat the rush. Weekday afternoons around 4:30 p.m. give you breathing room without sacrificing atmosphere.

## Step 3: Know the Drink Menu Before You Order

Miss B's happy hour cocktails focus on tropical spirits—rum, tequila, and house-made coconut syrups. The menu rotates seasonally, so what's on special this month might not be next month. Ask the bartender about the current lineup as soon as you sit down.

Look for drinks that showcase their tiki credentials: mai tais built with aged rum, mojitos with fresh mint, and signature coconut-forward cocktails you won't find at the [best bars mission beach](/blog/best-bars-mission-beach/). Happy hour pricing typically cuts 20–30% off regular prices, making $12–15 drinks drop to $8–11. That's the moment to try something you'd normally skip.

Don't overlook their flamingo tap wall—it's not just Instagram bait. House-made sodas, coconut water, and tropical juices behind that wall often become mixers for custom happy hour creations. Ask if the bartender can build a drink around something specific you see on tap.

## Step 4: Scope Out Shareable Plates and Appetizers

Happy hour food at Miss B's isn't an afterthought. The kitchen puts out shareable plates—think coconut shrimp, jerk chicken skewers, and island-inspired ceviche—at reduced prices during the window. These are meant to be passed around, so order two or three plates for a group of four.

The shareable format means you're not committing to a full entrée. You're grazing, sipping, and staying light enough to enjoy multiple cocktails without overloading. Ask your server which plates are currently on happy hour special and which ones pair best with the day's featured drinks.

If you're planning a larger gathering, consider whether Miss B's [private events mission beach](/blog/private-events-mission-beach/) setup might work better than standard happy hour seating. Group happy hours can book private patio sections, giving you dedicated space and a customized food and drink package.

## Step 5: Secure Your Preferred Seating

The fire pit patio is the crown jewel of Miss B's. If you want a table near the flames, arrive during the early-arrival window or call ahead to ask if they hold patio tables for happy hour reservations. Some bars do; some don't. Knowing Miss B's policy saves you from being seated inside when you came for that beachside fire pit experience.

If walk-in seating is first-come, first-served, head straight to the host stand and ask about patio availability. If nothing's open, ask how long the wait typically runs. A 10-minute wait is worth it; a 45-minute wait might not be, depending on your schedule.

The bar itself is a solid backup. You get direct access to the bartender, can watch the cocktail-building process, and often catch conversations with other regulars. Plus, you can see the flamingo tap wall up close—which, yes, is worth experiencing in person.

## Step 6: Order Strategically for Your Group

If you're going with friends, coordinate your order. One person ordering a single cocktail while others get full drinks and appetizers creates awkward table dynamics. Instead, commit to sharing plates, order at least one drink per person, and let the bartender know you're a group. They'll often pour a little extra or suggest a pitcher option for larger groups.

For solo happy hour visits, sit at the bar. You'll get faster service, better banter with the bartender, and a view of the entire room. Bartenders at tiki clubs love talking about their spirit selection and house recipes—use that to your advantage.

## Step 7: Time Your Second Round Strategically

Happy hour pricing typically ends at 6 or 7 p.m. If you're planning to stay longer, order your second round before the cutoff. Some bars honor the happy hour price if you order before the window closes, even if you sip slowly. Others cut off pricing immediately at closing time.

Ask your bartender about the exact policy. If you're close to the cutoff, order ahead. If you've got time, linger over your first drink and order the second one during happy hour pricing.

## Step 8: Explore the Broader Mission Beach Scene

If you're making an afternoon of it, don't limit yourself to one bar. Miss B's is part of a vibrant Mission Beach corridor. After happy hour, you could check out the [best brunch mission beach](/blog/best-brunch-mission-beach/) spots for late-afternoon bites, or explore [things to do mission beach](/blog/things-to-do-mission-beach/) like the boardwalk, shops, and beach access just steps away.

The proximity to the water means you can transition from happy hour into an evening walk, dinner at a neighboring restaurant, or heading back to your hotel. Mission Beach happy hour at Miss B's works as a standalone experience or as part of a larger beach day.

## Step 9: Check for Special Happy Hour Events

Miss B's occasionally runs themed happy hours—live music nights, seasonal tiki competitions, or ladies' nights with additional discounts. Follow their social media or call ahead to ask if anything special is happening on your planned visit day. A live ukulele player or DJ changes the entire vibe and makes the happy hour experience more memorable.

These events sometimes draw bigger crowds, so adjust your arrival time accordingly. If there's live music, arriving 30 minutes earlier than usual gives you better seating options.

## Step 10: Know When to Go Back

Happy hour is a standing invitation, not a one-time event. Miss B's runs it regularly, so if you miss this week's window, there's another one coming. But if you find a day and time that works—say, Thursday at 4:30 p.m.—bookmark it mentally. Consistency beats chasing specials across multiple bars.

The best happy hours feel like you're part of the regular crowd, not a tourist hunting for deals. Return at the same time, order something similar, and the bartender will start remembering your name. That's when happy hour stops being transactional and becomes part of your Mission Beach routine.
