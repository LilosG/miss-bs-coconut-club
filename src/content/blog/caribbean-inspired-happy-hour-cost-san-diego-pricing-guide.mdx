Planning a Caribbean-inspired happy hour in San Diego without breaking the bank? Miss B's Coconut Club on Mission Boulevard delivers tiki-club energy, island cocktails, and shareable plates at prices that won't drain your wallet. But knowing exactly what you'll spend—and how it stacks up against other Caribbean venues in the area—takes the guesswork out of the experience. This guide breaks down Miss B's happy hour costs, compares them to competitors, and shows you how to maximize value whether you're solo or with a group.

## Understanding Miss B's Happy Hour Window and Baseline Pricing

Miss B's Coconut Club runs happy hour daily from 4–6 PM, a two-hour window that captures the post-work crowd and early dinner guests. During these hours, the fire pit patio becomes the social hub of Mission Beach, and pricing shifts to encourage longer stays and group gatherings.

The baseline happy hour pricing at Miss B's breaks into three categories: cocktails, draft beer, and food. Rum-forward cocktails—the backbone of Caribbean happy hour—start at $8 and climb to $12 for signature drinks and premium spirit selections. This is the entry point for most visitors. Draft beer specials run $4–$5 per pour, depending on the selection. Food pricing ranges from $6 for light bites to $18 for shareable appetizers designed for two to three people.

Compared to standard San Diego bar pricing, these rates represent a 30–40% discount from dinner-hour pricing at the same venue. A cocktail that costs $14–$16 after 6 PM drops to $8–$10 during happy hour. That difference compounds quickly when you're planning a group outing.

## Rum Cocktail Breakdown: What You'll Actually Pay

The rum cocktails at Miss B's are the real draw during happy hour, and pricing is transparent and fair. The house rum cocktails—think mojitos, daiquiris, and the signature Coconut Club punch—land in the $8–$10 range. These drinks use standard Caribbean rums and fresh citrus, made to order, not pre-batched. You're paying for quality ingredients and technique, not just the brand name.

Mid-tier rum cocktails, including those made with aged or spiced rums, run $10–$12. These might feature premium brands like Appleton Estate or Diplomatico and often include house-made syrups or infusions. If you're a rum enthusiast, this tier offers the best value during happy hour—you'd pay $16–$18 for the same drink at 7 PM.

The flamingo tap wall at Miss B's also features rotating Caribbean-inspired cocktails that shift seasonally. These specialty drinks occasionally command the higher $12 price point but are worth it if you want something beyond the standard repertoire. Ask your server what's new; happy hour is the perfect time to experiment without the full dinner-hour markup.

For budget-conscious visitors, the simple rum and coke or rum and ginger beer sits at $6–$8, undercutting even the draft beer specials and delivering pure island vibes without pretense.

## Draft Beer and Non-Cocktail Options

If rum cocktails aren't your thing, Miss B's happy hour draft beer selection keeps costs low while maintaining quality. Expect $4–$5 per pour on a rotating selection of lagers, IPAs, and seasonal brews. Local San Diego breweries often feature during happy hour, so you're supporting regional businesses while staying under $5 per drink.

Canned and bottled beer options start at $5 and go up to $7 for premium imports, still well below regular pricing. Non-alcoholic options—virgin mojitos, fresh-squeezed citrus drinks, and specialty sodas—run $4–$6, making Miss B's accessible to non-drinkers without forcing them into overpriced soft-drink territory.

The value play here is combining a draft beer with a shareable appetizer. A $4.50 beer plus a $15 appetizer split between two people costs $9.75 per person—less than a single cocktail elsewhere.

## Shareable Appetizers and Food Costs

Happy hour food at Miss B's is designed for grazing and sharing, which is where the real value emerges. Shareable plates range from $12–$18 and are portioned for 2–3 people. Think crispy plantain chips with house-made dips, jerk chicken skewers, or Caribbean ceviche. These plates are substantial enough to constitute a light dinner, especially when paired with a drink or two.

Individual bites—empanadas, conch fritters, or coconut shrimp—run $6–$9 each. If you're solo or prefer smaller portions, ordering two or three bites alongside a cocktail keeps your total under $20 and lets you sample multiple flavor profiles.

## Maximizing Value: Strategies for Groups and Solo Visitors

**For groups of 3–4:** Order 2–3 shareable appetizer plates and rotate cocktails. Total per-person cost: $15–$22. This approach lets everyone try different drinks and food without overcommitting to a single item. The shareable model also extends happy hour by encouraging conversation and slower consumption.

**For couples:** One shareable plate + two cocktails = $24–$32 total, or $12–$16 per person. This is a full, satisfying happy hour experience without the dinner-hour sticker shock.

**For solo visitors:** Sit at the bar, order a single cocktail and a bite, and stay for 45 minutes to an hour. Total cost: $12–$18. The bartenders at Miss B's are conversational and knowledgeable about rum, making solo happy hours social rather than isolating.

**Timing strategy:** Arrive at 4:15 PM rather than 4:00 PM. You'll avoid the immediate post-work rush, get faster service, and have more seating options on the patio. The 5:45–6:00 PM window is the sweet spot if you want to linger without feeling pressured to order dinner; most groups have cleared out, and the kitchen is winding down.
