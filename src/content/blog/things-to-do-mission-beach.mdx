Mission Beach is one of the easiest San Diego coastal neighborhoods to turn into a full day. The Pacific Ocean is on one side of the peninsula, Mission Bay is on the other, and Mission Blvd runs through the middle with restaurants, bars and local businesses.

You do not need an overbuilt itinerary. Pick a few stops that fit together geographically and leave enough time to walk the neighborhood.

## 1. Start at the Pacific Beach and Boardwalk

The ocean side is the most obvious first stop. Mission Beach's boardwalk is a flat route used by walkers, runners, cyclists and skaters, with direct access to the sand along the way.

A morning walk is useful if you want to see the neighborhood before the busiest part of the day. If the beach is the main reason for your visit, build the rest of the itinerary around it rather than trying to squeeze it between restaurant reservations.

## 2. Visit Belmont Park

Belmont Park is one of Mission Beach's most recognizable landmarks and sits directly along Mission Blvd near the boardwalk.

The park is built around the historic Giant Dipper wooden roller coaster and includes rides, games and other attractions. Operating hours can vary, so check Belmont Park's current information before planning the day around a specific attraction.

## 3. See the Mission Bay Side

Walk east across the peninsula and the atmosphere changes quickly. Mission Bay is calmer than the ocean side and is surrounded by paths, parks and recreation areas.

That contrast is part of what makes Mission Beach unusual: ocean surf and calm bay water are only a short distance apart.

## 4. Get Brunch on Mission Blvd

[Miss B's Coconut Club](/brunch) at **3704 Mission Blvd** serves brunch every day until 2pm.

Current brunch options include breakfast burritos, breakfast tacos, French toast, biscuits and gravy, an açaí bowl and breakfast sandwiches, along with mimosas and morning cocktails.

Weekday service begins at 10am and weekend service begins at 9am.

## 5. Explore the Open-Air Bar and Patio

If you are visiting later in the day, Miss B's [open-air bar and patio](/space) offer a place to stop without leaving the Mission Beach neighborhood.

The venue includes a tropical dining room, an outdoor patio and the signature wall of 24 copper flamingo tap handles. The [food and drink menu](/menu) runs beyond brunch with burgers, tacos, wings, seafood and Caribbean-inspired dishes.

## 6. Walk or Bike Instead of Moving the Car

One of Mission Beach's advantages is that a large part of the neighborhood can be explored without repeatedly driving and parking.

If you are staying nearby, walking or biking is often the simplest way to combine the beach, boardwalk, bay and Mission Blvd. If you do drive, give yourself extra time during busy beach periods and confirm current parking rules wherever you leave the car.

## 7. Stay for Weekday Happy Hour or Dinner

Miss B's current happy hour runs **Monday through Friday from 3pm to 5pm**. Dinner and bar service continue afterward.

If your Mission Beach day starts with the beach, a late lunch, happy hour or dinner can be an easier finish than trying to schedule everything around one fixed midday meal.

Check the [FAQ](/faq) for current happy-hour and operating details.

## 8. Plan a Group Gathering in Mission Beach

For birthdays, company outings, bachelorette groups and other celebrations, Miss B's also offers [private-event options](/private-events) including indoor and outdoor configurations and full venue buyouts based on guest count and availability.

That can be useful if Mission Beach is the setting for the event rather than just one stop during the day.

## A Simple Mission Beach Itinerary

**Morning:** Beach or boardwalk  
**Late morning:** Belmont Park or Mission Bay  
**Brunch/lunch:** Miss B's Coconut Club  
**Afternoon:** Beach, bike ride or bay  
**Late afternoon:** Happy hour on a weekday or cocktails  
**Evening:** Dinner in Mission Beach or continue north toward Pacific Beach
