San Diego's rum cocktail scene thrives in pockets of the city where tiki culture meets Caribbean tradition. Whether you're hunting for a shareable punch bowl, a craft sipping rum, or a tropical escape with your friends, the best rum cocktails here balance playful presentation with serious spirit selection. We've mapped out where to find them—from Mission Beach's open-air fire pits to Little Italy's craft-focused bars.

## Miss B's Coconut Club: Mission Beach's Rum Punch Headquarters

Start at the source: Miss B's Coconut Club sits at 3704 Mission Blvd with a fire pit patio, a flamingo tap wall, and a rum program built for groups. Their Coconut Fire Punch arrives in a shared bowl—a float of overproof rum ignites tableside, and the base blends aged Caribbean rum with fresh coconut cream, lime, and a hint of tiki bitters. This is the drink that defines shareable rum culture in Mission Beach.

The bar's rum selection leans into Caribbean producers: Appleton Estate from Jamaica, Mount Gay from Barbados, and Diplomatico from Venezuela sit alongside California craft options. Single-serve cocktails like their Dark & Stormy use Gosling's Black Seal, while the Mojito riff swaps white rum for an aged agricole that carries grass and mineral notes. The open-air setting—palm fronds, ocean breeze, tiki torches—makes the entire experience feel like you've left San Diego without leaving Mission Beach.

Miss B's also anchors the [best bars mission beach](/blog/best-bars-mission-beach/) scene, drawing locals and visitors who want rum-forward drinks without pretension. Weekend crowds arrive early; the bar fills fastest between 5 and 7 p.m.

## The Tiki Bar: Pacific Beach's Tropical Anchor

Cross into Pacific Beach and The Tiki Bar (4427 Ocean Blvd) maintains the old-school tiki playbook. Carved wooden faces, bamboo accents, and a full arsenal of tropical rums create an immersive island atmosphere. Their Mai Tai uses a blend of aged Jamaican and white rums, balanced with orgeat and lime—a textbook version that lets the rum speak.

The Scorpion Bowl, a shareable punch in a ceramic vessel, combines light and dark rums with fruit juices and a float of 151-proof rum. It's designed for groups and carries the warning label of all effective communal cocktails: pace yourself. The bar also stocks rare and vintage rums for sippers who want to move beyond mixed drinks. Plantation Stiggins' Fancy Pineapple Rum and Rhum Agricole selections appeal to collectors and curious drinkers alike.

## Catania: Little Italy's Craft Rum Focus

In Little Italy, Catania (1610 India St) takes a more refined approach to rum cocktails. The bar program emphasizes single-origin rums and smaller producers—Foursquare from Barbados, Hampden from Jamaica, and limited-release bottlings rotate through the menu. Their Daiquiri uses a specific rum chosen for the season, allowing the spirit's terroir to dominate the drink.

Catania's rum Old Fashioned swaps bourbon for an aged Caribbean rum, building complexity with bitters and a large ice cube. The environment—dim lighting, wood and leather, craft-focused service—appeals to drinkers who want education alongside their cocktail. This is where you go if you're serious about understanding rum, not just consuming it.

## Puesto: Mission Valley's Caribbean Influence

Puesto (multiple San Diego locations; the Mission Valley branch is at 7755 Hazard Center Dr) weaves rum cocktails into a larger Mexican and Caribbean menu. Their rum program focuses on sipping spirits and classic tropical drinks. The Mojito here uses fresh mint from local suppliers, and the rum selection includes both Caribbean staples and craft options from craft distilleries.

The bar's location in Mission Valley makes it accessible for those outside the beach neighborhoods. Daytime happy hours feature rum punch specials, and the patio setting (weather-dependent) captures some of the open-air energy that defines San Diego's best rum bars.

## The Taco Stand's Rum Selections

While The Taco Stand (multiple locations) operates primarily as a casual food spot, several locations maintain solid rum cocktail programs. Their Rum Runner combines light rum, coconut rum, banana liqueur, and pineapple juice—a gateway drink for newcomers to rum cocktails. The casual vibe and late-night hours (some locations stay open until 2 a.m.) make it a practical stop after dinner or a night out.

## Why San Diego's Rum Scene Stands Out

San Diego's rum cocktail bars benefit from year-round weather that supports outdoor drinking, a strong tiki heritage rooted in mid-century beach culture, and access to Caribbean spirits through the port. The city's proximity to Mexico also influences menu design—many bars blend Caribbean and Latin American rum traditions.

The [tropical cocktails san diego](/blog/tropical-cocktails-san-diego/) market has grown beyond tourist traps. Bartenders here study rum's origins, source small-batch producers, and treat punch bowls as serious craft rather than novelty items. Miss B's Coconut Club exemplifies this shift: the fire pit and flamingo wall draw Instagram attention, but the rum selection and technique keep people returning.

## Planning Your Rum Crawl

Start early afternoon at Miss B's for the Coconut Fire Punch while the sun is high and the fire pit is active. Move to The Tiki Bar in Pacific Beach by early evening—the Scorpion Bowl works best when you have time to linger. If you're building a full day, consider pairing rum cocktails with food: Puesto offers Caribbean-influenced plates, and several Mission Beach spots serve food alongside drinks.

For groups planning celebrations, [bachelorette party san diego](/blog/bachelorette-party-san-diego/) venues often highlight Miss B's and The Tiki Bar as top choices for shared cocktail experiences. Reserve ahead during weekends; both bars fill quickly.
