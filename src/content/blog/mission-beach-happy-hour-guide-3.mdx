Happy hour in Mission Beach doesn't mean settling for watered-down drinks and stale bar snacks. At Miss B's Coconut Club on Mission Boulevard, you're getting tiki-club energy, fire pit seating, and cocktails that actually taste like they belong on a beach—all at prices that won't wreck your week. This guide walks you through the exact steps to maximize your happy hour experience, from timing your arrival to ordering the right shareable plates.

## Step 1: Know the Happy Hour Window and Plan Your Timing

Miss B's happy hour runs **4–6 PM daily**. That two-hour window is your golden ticket, but timing matters more than you might think.

**The honest truth:** If you show up at 4:15 PM on a Friday or Saturday during summer, you're fighting for a seat. The patio—especially the high-top tables near the fire pit—fills within the first 15 minutes. The flamingo tap wall, a visual anchor of the space, becomes a social magnet fast.

**Best timing strategy:**
- **Weekdays (Monday–Thursday):** Arrive anytime between 4–5:30 PM. You'll find seating without the crush, and the vibe is still lively.
- **Fridays:** Get there by 4:10 PM or wait until 5:45 PM when tables start turning over.
- **Saturdays and Sundays:** Arrive at 3:50 PM (yes, five minutes early) or plan for 5:30 PM onward. Summer weekends are packed; off-season Saturday afternoons are more forgiving.

If you're planning a group of four or more, skip ahead to Step 3 about reservations. Solo or pairs can usually grab bar seating or a high-top without calling first.

## Step 2: Review the Drink Menu Before You Arrive

Miss B's happy hour cocktails get $2 off the regular price. That's significant when you're ordering rounds. The menu rotates seasonally, but the core tiki drinks stay consistent.

**Must-order happy hour cocktails:**
- **Coconut Club Mai Tai:** The house signature. Rum-forward, balanced citrus, and a float that makes it Instagram-worthy. At happy hour pricing, it's a steal.
- **Tropical Punch:** A shareable-style drink served in a large format. Great for splitting between two people.
- **Dark & Stormy:** If you want something straightforward, this rum-and-ginger-beer combo is flawless.
- **Piña Colada:** Sounds basic, but Miss B's version uses fresh coconut cream and doesn't lean syrupy.

**Beer and wine:** Domestic beers drop to $3–$4, and wine pours are $1–$2 off. If you're not in a cocktail mood, the beer selection includes lighter options perfect for an afternoon transition into evening.

**Pro tip:** Check the specials board when you arrive. Miss B's occasionally runs limited-time happy hour features—last month they offered $5 appetizer-cocktail pairings on Tuesdays.

## Step 3: Secure Your Spot (Reservation or Walk-In Strategy)

Here's where strategy separates a good happy hour from a frustrating one.

**For walk-ins:**
Call Miss B's at the Mission Boulevard location about 30 minutes before you plan to arrive. Ask if they have patio seating available. The staff will give you an honest answer. If they say "packed," you have two choices: come back in 45 minutes or head to the bar. The bar is genuinely fun—you're surrounded by the flamingo tap wall, and bartenders are chatty.

**For groups of 4+:**
Make a reservation. Miss B's takes them for happy hour, and a table reservation guarantees you're not standing around waiting. Call or use their online system if available. Mention if you want patio seating; they'll note it.

**For larger groups (8+):**
If you're organizing a team outing or casual group dinner that extends into happy hour, ask about semi-private patio sections. Miss B's can often cordon off a corner of the fire pit area. This is especially useful if you're combining happy hour with a larger meal.

## Step 4: Order the Right Shareable Plates

Happy hour food at Miss B's isn't an afterthought. The kitchen runs a separate happy hour menu with plates designed for sharing and priced between $8–$12.

**Top shareable plates:**
- **Crispy Coconut Shrimp:** Fried shrimp with a coconut crust, served with a sweet chili sauce. Order this first. It's gone fast.
- **Jerk Chicken Skewers:** Three skewers of marinated chicken with a kick. Pairs perfectly with Mai Tais.
- **Island Nachos:** Tortilla chips, pulled pork, jalapeños, queso, and a lime crema. Feeds two people comfortably.
- **Fish Tacos:** Three tacos with fresh catch, cabbage slaw, and a citrus aioli. Lighter option if you want to eat without getting too full.
- **Conch Fritters:** Fried dough balls with conch meat inside. Carb-heavy but absolutely worth it.

**Ordering strategy:** For a group of two, order one plate and one cocktail each. For four people, order two plates, four cocktails, and you're set for the full two hours. For six or more, order three plates and pace your drinks.

**Honest note:** The kitchen gets busy during peak happy hour. If you order at 5:45 PM on a Friday, food might take 20–25 minutes. Order early or be patient. It's worth the wait.

## Step 5: Scope Out Seating and Pick Your Spot

Miss B's layout is open-air, which means weather matters and seating preferences vary.

**Patio by the fire pit:** The premium spot. Tables have views of the beach-facing side of Mission Boulevard, and the fire pit creates a social atmosphere. Slightly warmer in the evening. Best for groups celebrating or lingering.

**Flamingo tap wall:** The bar area with the iconic pink flamingo tap handles. Perfect for solo diners or pairs. You get bartender banter and can watch the drink-making action.

**High-top tables near the entrance:** Good compromise. You're not tucked into the patio but still get outdoor vibes without the fire pit heat.

**Indoor seating:** Available if the patio is full or if the evening is cold. It's cozier but loses the open-air tiki-club energy that makes Miss B's special.

**Timing note:** Sunset happy hours (5–6 PM on clear days) are golden. The light hits the beach, and the patio becomes the place to be.

## Step 6: Maximize Your Happy Hour Duration

The happy hour window is two hours. Here's how to use it fully without feeling rushed.

**Timeline for a pair:**
- **4:00–4:20 PM:** Arrive, get seated, order first round of cocktails and one shareable plate.
- **4:20–4:50 PM:** Eat, chat, enjoy first drinks.
- **4:50–5:15 PM:** Order second round of cocktails and a second plate if you're hungry.
- **5:15–6:00 PM:** Finish second round, settle up, or transition into dinner if you're staying longer.

**Timeline for a group of 4:**
- **4:05–4:25 PM:** Arrive, get seated, order two plates and four cocktails.
- **4:25–5:00 PM:** Eat, settle into conversation.
- **5:00–5:30 PM:** Order second round of cocktails (happy hour pricing ends at 6 PM, so time this right).
- **5:30–6:00 PM:** Finish drinks, decide if you're staying for dinner or heading out.

**Pro tip:** If you want to stay past 6 PM, the transition is smooth. The dinner menu kicks in, prices normalize, but the vibe doesn't change. Many groups use happy hour as the entry point and naturally stay for a full meal.

## Step 8: Settle Up and Understand the Tab

Happy hour pricing is straightforward. Cocktails are $2 off, beer and wine are discounted, and food is on the happy hour menu. There's no hidden markup or surprise charges.

**Typical tab for two people (two hours):**
- Two cocktails at happy hour pricing: $20–$24
- Two shareable plates: $16–$24
- Tax and tip: $8–$10
- **Total: $44–$58**

**For four people (two hours):**
- Four cocktails: $40–$48
- Two shareable plates: $16–$24
- Tax and tip: $12–$16
- **Total: $68–$88**

Tipping at happy hour is the same as regular service—15–20% is standard. The bartenders and servers are working just as hard during the rush.
